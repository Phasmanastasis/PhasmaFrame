# Problem & Market Opportunity

> Source issue: **PHASM-11**. Parent: PHASM-7. Milestone: MVP.

Grounds the need for an offline hypertension follow-up workflow. **All findings below are
provisional.** The team's personal case context and the Quick research synthesis are
signals, not population-level proof. No prevalence or impact statistic is asserted as
fact.

## Carlos matrix (current state)

- **Real** — Hypertension and stroke are real health concerns. Case notes and the Quick
  synthesis are signals, not independent validation of *this specific* workflow gap.
- **Large** — **Unknown** for this product segment. Any prevalence or population figure
  must be sourced from authoritative references before use.
- **Significant** — The workflow aims to preserve dated readings and make them reviewable
  by a BHW. Health impact *from the app* is unproven.
- **Relevant** — Fits the competition's Philippine community-care and chronic-disease
  track; targets low-connectivity / far-flung contexts.
- **Urgent** — Access, continuity, and medicine-availability concerns appear in the team's
  case context. Frequency and current severity across target communities **need
  validation**.

## Provisional 5 Whys

1. **Why build follow-up support?** A person managing hypertension needs readings and
   visit history to remain usable over time.
2. **Why might the history be hard to use?** Entries can stay on a household device or
   paper between visits.
3. **Why does that matter?** A BHW may not have the patient's recent record during a
   visit.
4. **Why can the handoff fail?** Far-flung visits and unreliable connectivity make live
   access unreliable.
5. **Why focus on an offline transfer?** A deliberate device-to-device handoff can carry
   the record without internet.

> Validate this chain with BHWs and RHU staff before treating it as established.

## Root-problem hypothesis

Low-connectivity community follow-up lacks a simple, practical way to bring a patient's
recorded blood-pressure history to a BHW for review. **This is a hypothesis, not a
validated finding.**
